We use Sentry to receive crash reports and diagnostic data (such as device model, operating-system version, App version, and the technical context of an error) so we can fix problems. Our integration is configured with personal-data collection disabled by default (no default personal identifiers, no automatic screenshots, no view-hierarchy capture), and events are filtered before they are sent.

Purpose: to keep the App stable and secure and to improve it. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in a reliable, secure App (Art. 6(1)(f)).

3.4 Data that stays on your device (not collected by us)

The following are stored only on your device in the App’s local storage and are never transmitted to us by the App: your in-App browsing history, your default page and other settings, and the technical log described below. The object-detection models run locally on your device. Deleting the App removes this local data.

Findings and their screenshots are stored on your device as well — but they are not only on your device once you sign in and agree to back them up. That case is described in section 3.5.

The App also keeps a technical log of what it was doing — which can include web addresses, file names and identifiers — so that a support report can describe a problem you hit earlier rather than only the moment you wrote in. It is stored only on your device, is capped at five megabytes with the oldest entries discarded first, and entries older than seven days are removed automatically. It is never sent anywhere on its own: it leaves your device only if you attach it to a support email yourself, and the attachment is redacted unless you expressly choose “Send unredacted data”. You can switch the log off under Settings → ML Browser → About the app → Debug → “Keep a detailed log”, which also deletes what has been stored, and clearing your browsing history deletes it too.

You may choose to send some of it to us yourself. If you contact support from the App, the App prepares an email — containing technical details such as your App version, device model and operating-system version, and, where relevant, the web address involved in the problem — and you may attach a screenshot you took and selected. You may also choose to attach the latest part of the App’s redacted technical log (up to 5 MiB); it is off by default and can help us diagnose what happened before a problem. If you expressly enable Send unredacted data, the log may instead include complete URLs, identifiers, location data, and other traceable technical details. You review that email and send it from your own email account; you can edit the email, remove any attachment, or not send it at all. Nothing is sent unless you send it.

3.5 Findings you back up to your account

If you are signed in, the App can back up the detections you save (“findings”) and their screenshots to your account, so that a season of fieldwork survives a lost, broken or replaced phone. This is what the account is for.

It does not happen silently. While you are signed out, nothing is uploaded. When you first sign in with findings already on the device, the App asks whether to back them up and does nothing until you answer — if you decline, those findings stay on your device only. Findings you create afterwards while signed in are backed up as you make them.

What is uploaded is the finding as you see it: its title, your note and label, the finder credit if you filled one in, the web address of the map or page it came from, where on that page the detection sits, any coordinates you typed in yourself, the date, and the screenshot image.

Where it is stored. With Cloudflare, under a location belonging to your account, and our server checks on every single request that the account asking for data is the account that owns it. Two parts are stored differently, and we would rather be exact about it:

  • The findings and screenshots themselves — everything you wrote and captured — are held in object storage we have pinned to the European Union. Their contents stay in the EU.
  • A small index recording which findings your account has, when each was last changed, how large it is, and which have been deleted, is held in Cloudflare’s database service, which does not offer a region setting. It may therefore be processed outside the EU, under the safeguards described in section 6. It holds no titles, notes, coordinates or images — only identifiers, timestamps and sizes.

Who can read it. It is encrypted in transit and encrypted at rest by Cloudflare, but it is not end-to-end encrypted: we hold the keys to that storage, so we are technically able to read your findings and screenshots. We access them only where we have a reason to — answering a support request you sent us, investigating a security incident, or checking that a deletion really happened — and not otherwise. We chose this over end-to-end encryption deliberately: with end-to-end encryption, losing your key would mean losing your fieldwork for good, with nothing we could do to help you recover it.

Deleting it. Deleting a finding in the App deletes it from your backup too, on every device. Deleting your account removes every finding and screenshot we hold for you. Note that your backup is the one part of your data that uninstalling the App does not remove — that is exactly what makes restoring on a new phone possible — so delete your account if you want it gone.

Storage limits. Each account has a storage allowance. If you reach it the App tells you and stops uploading new findings; nothing already backed up is removed, and nothing on your device is affected.

Purpose: to back up your findings to your account and restore them on your devices. Legal basis: performance of a contract (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)) — it is the service you signed in for. Processor: Cloudflare, Inc. (cloudflare.com/privacypolicy).

ML Browser is a web browser. When you visit a website, that website (and any third parties it loads) may receive data from your device as in any browser, governed by their own privacy policies. We do not automatically send a record of the websites you visit. A support log you explicitly choose to attach removes complete web addresses, identifiers, locations, and similar traceable details by default. If you expressly choose to send unredacted data, it may contain those complete details involved in the problem.

Whichever of those you choose, the attached log is encrypted before it leaves your device and can be opened only by ML Browser support. You keep the choice of whether to attach it and can remove it from the email before sending, but — unlike the rest of the message, which you can read in full — you cannot read the log's contents. The trade is that nobody else can either, including anyone the email is forwarded to or anyone who intercepts it.

8. Telemetry

If enabled, ML Browser uses TelemetryDeck to understand high-level, aggregated app usage and improve the App. Optional usage analytics is off by default; it starts only after you make an explicit choice in the App, and you can change that choice at any time in Settings → Privacy.

Each usage signal carries an identifier so that repeated signals can be recognized as coming from the same user. If you turn analytics on while you are signed in, that identifier is derived from your account identifier, so your usage is counted once across all the devices you sign in on. If you are signed out, or have not turned analytics on for your account, any usage data that is sent uses an anonymous per-installation identifier instead — a value your device generates for this App that is not connected to your account. In either case TelemetryDeck’s software converts the value into a hashed form on your device before any signal is sent: the underlying identifier itself is never transmitted in plain form.

Because the account-derived identifier is stable over time, optional usage data is pseudonymous rather than anonymous, and we treat it as personal data.

Your analytics choice is recorded per account: if a different account signs in on the same device, it is asked separately and analytics stays off for that account until it makes its own choice. When you sign out, the App immediately stops associating any further usage with your account — nothing you do while signed out is linked to it.

We do not send page content, screenshots, images, detection results, URLs, page titles, filenames, model names, account names, email addresses, or advertising identifiers. We do not use analytics for advertising, cross-app tracking, or sale of personal data.

TelemetryDeck processes this optional usage data on our behalf. TelemetryDeck states that its usage-data infrastructure is located in the European Union. More information is available from TelemetryDeck at telemetrydeck.com/privacy.

When you turn analytics off, the App stops sending future optional analytics signals and clears locally stored analytics data where possible. Turning analytics off does not delete information already received by TelemetryDeck; you may contact us to request help with a deletion request.

Our basis for processing this optional usage data is your active analytics setting in the App. You may change that setting at any time without affecting the App’s core functionality.

9. Security

We use technical and organizational measures designed to protect your data, including encryption in transit and encryption at rest. For the findings you back up, our server authorizes every request against the signed-in account, so one account cannot read another’s data, and the storage holding their contents is pinned to the European Union. As explained in section 3.5, that backup is not end-to-end encrypted, so we are able to read it; access on our side is limited to support, security and deletion checks. No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, so we cannot guarantee absolute security.
